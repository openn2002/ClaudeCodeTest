## Brief 1: "GLP-1s Are Causing Muscle Loss — Here's What the Science Says You Should Do About It"

**Platform:** TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts (multi-platform)
**Format:** Credentialed expert myth-bust / data reveal
**Target length:** 45–75 seconds (short-form); optional 3–5 minute extended cut for YouTube
**Market:** Both — produce as two branded variants (CSIRO TWD for AU; Mayo Clinic Diet for US)
**Audience:** Adults 35–55 currently using or seriously considering GLP-1 medications; frustrated by the absence of structured dietary guidance from their prescriber; active on Reddit, TikTok, and health news sites

### Hook (First 3–5 seconds)

ON SCREEN TEXT: "Up to 40% of weight lost on GLP-1 medications is lean muscle."
SPOKEN: "If you're on Ozempic or Mounjaro and nobody has told you this yet — you need to hear it."

Alternative hooks to test:
1. "Only 43% of people on GLP-1 medications are eating enough protein. Here's why that matters — and exactly how much you actually need."
2. "Everyone's talking about how much weight Ozempic helps you lose. Nobody's talking about what kind of weight."

### Video Structure

1. **Hook (0–5 sec):** Dietitian to camera. Stat on screen. No music — silence forces attention.
2. **The problem, plainly stated (5–20 sec):** "When you lose weight rapidly — which GLP-1 medications can cause — your body doesn't just lose fat. It can also break down muscle. Studies suggest up to 40% of the weight lost on GLP-1s may be lean mass. And muscle isn't just about how you look — it's your metabolism, your strength, and your long-term weight maintenance."
3. **The science credential moment (20–30 sec):** "A joint advisory from four major medical associations — released earlier this year — recommends that people on GLP-1 medications aim for 1.2 to 1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight, every day. Most people aren't close." [AU: "That's where the CSIRO Total Wellbeing Diet's higher-protein framework was built for exactly this situation." | US: "That's exactly what the Mayo Clinic Diet's Weight-Loss Medications Program was designed to address."]
4. **The specific, actionable number (30–50 sec):** "For an 80kg person, that means roughly 96 to 128 grams of protein a day. This isn't about eating more. It's about eating smarter, especially when GLP-1 medications are reducing your appetite and your food volume."
5. **Credibility close (50–65 sec):** "This is what structured, science-backed programs are built for. Not a generic calorie count. A protein-first framework that protects your muscle while the medication does its job." [CSIRO / Mayo Clinic program name and logo on screen.]
6. **CTA (65–75 sec):** [AU] "The CSIRO Total Wellbeing Diet now works alongside weight loss medications. Search CSIRO Total Wellbeing Diet or tap the link to start." [US] "The Mayo Clinic Diet has a dedicated Weight-Loss Medications Program. Link in bio."

### Key Messages
- GLP-1 medications can cause muscle loss, not just fat loss — this is a legitimate medical concern, not a scare story.
- The clinical recommendation from leading medical associations is specific: 1.2–1.6g protein per kilogram bodyweight per day.
- Most GLP-1 users are not meeting this threshold and have no structured plan to do so.
- The CSIRO TWD / Mayo Clinic Diet's higher-protein, structured frameworks are the solution — built by institutional science, not a startup.

### Science / Credibility Anchors
- "Up to 40% of weight lost on GLP-1 medications may be lean muscle mass" — Harvard Science Review, February 2026
- "Joint advisory from ACLM, ASN, Obesity Medicine Association, and the Obesity Society (early 2026): recommends 1.2–1.6g protein/kg/day for GLP-1 users"
- "Only 43% of GLP-1 users are meeting minimum protein intake thresholds"
- CSIRO TWD: Higher-protein, low-GI program backed by peer-reviewed CSIRO research
- Mayo Clinic Diet: Weight-Loss Medications Program — built on Mayo Clinic clinical expertise

### Call to Action
- AU: Visit totalwellbeingdiet.com.au — start a free trial or explore the program.
- US: Visit diet.mayoclinic.org — explore the Weight-Loss Medications Program.
- Both: "Save this if you or someone you know is on a GLP-1 medication." (Save prompt drives algorithmic reach.)

### Why This Will Perform
This concept targets the single highest-anxiety GLP-1 question that Reddit, TikTok, and health news are generating right now — muscle loss — and delivers an answer no competitor can match with this level of clinical authority. WW failed its GLP-1 content pivot because it had no science voice. Juniper is restricted from naming medications. Noom has no clinical nutrition depth. The specific protein numbers give the audience something concrete to act on, which drives saves — the most durable engagement signal.

### Production Notes
- Film two branded variants in one shoot: CSIRO TWD (AU) and Mayo Clinic Diet (US). Same script structure, different credential callout and program name.
- Dietitian on camera: natural lighting, clean but warm background. No lab coat needed.
- On-screen text should display the key statistics as the dietitian speaks them — serves viewers watching without sound.
- Do not use dramatic music or fear-adjacent visuals. Frame the muscle loss finding as information, not alarm.
- Consider a 3-minute extended version for YouTube walking through a sample high-protein day for a GLP-1 user — serves the "what to eat on Ozempic" keyword simultaneously.
- Strong series anchor: follow-up episodes could cover "Protein on a small appetite," "What happens when you stop," and "Exercise for muscle protection on GLP-1s."

## Brief 2: "Intermittent Fasting Doesn't Work — A 22-Study Review Just Proved It. Here's What Does."

**Platform:** TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts (primary); Facebook Reels (secondary)
**Format:** Credentialed expert myth-bust / news reaction
**Target length:** 45–60 seconds (short-form)
**Market:** Both — CSIRO TWD variant (AU) and Mayo Clinic Diet variant (US)
**Audience:** Adults 40–65 who have tried intermittent fasting, had mixed results, and are now reconsidering their approach. Also serves the science-credibility seeker who actively follows nutrition research news.

### Hook (First 3–5 seconds)

ON SCREEN TEXT: "A review of 22 studies just found intermittent fasting produces little to no difference vs. regular dieting."
SPOKEN: "If you've been doing intermittent fasting and wondering why you're not getting results — science just backed you up."

Alternative hooks to test:
1. "You didn't fail intermittent fasting. Intermittent fasting failed you — and now there are 22 studies to prove it."
2. "As a [CSIRO / Mayo Clinic] dietitian, I'm not surprised by the new Cochrane Review on intermittent fasting. Here's what the evidence has been pointing to for years."

### Video Structure

1. **Hook (0–5 sec):** Dietitian to camera. Cochrane Review finding on screen as bold text.
2. **What the study found (5–20 sec):** "The Cochrane Collaboration — which produces the most rigorous systematic reviews in medicine — just published an analysis of 22 randomised controlled trials involving nearly 2,000 participants. The finding: intermittent fasting produces little to no difference compared to continuous calorie restriction for weight loss or health outcomes. This is not a fringe finding. This is the gold standard of evidence."
3. **The validation moment (20–30 sec):** "If you've tried 16:8 or 5:2 and struggled — the problem wasn't your willpower. The structure itself doesn't have an evidence advantage. What the evidence does consistently support is total dietary quality and adequate protein."
4. **What the science actually supports (30–50 sec):** [AU] "The CSIRO Total Wellbeing Diet was built on decades of research into what produces sustainable results: higher protein, low-GI foods, and a structure you can follow for life — not a fasting window. The average member loses 7.2% of their body weight in 12 weeks." [US] "The Mayo Clinic Diet is built on 15 evidence-based habits — not a clock. Members lose 6 to 10 pounds in the first 2 weeks, and the habits are designed to last a lifetime."
5. **CTA (50–60 sec):** "If you're ready to try something with decades of peer-reviewed research behind it — not just a trending format — the link is in the bio." Program name and logo on screen.

### Key Messages
- A landmark Cochrane Review (22 RCTs, ~2,000 participants, February 2026) found intermittent fasting produces little to no additional benefit versus standard dieting.
- This is not a knock on people who tried IF — it is a validation of why many found it unsustainable.
- The evidence consistently supports dietary quality, protein adequacy, and sustainable structure — exactly what CSIRO TWD and Mayo Clinic Diet are built on.
- Institutional science has always pointed here. This news cycle is simply catching up.

### Science / Credibility Anchors
- Cochrane Collaboration systematic review, February 2026: 22 RCTs, approximately 2,000 participants, finding: IF produces "little to no difference" versus standard calorie restriction
- CSIRO TWD: "Average member loses 7.2% of body weight after 12 weeks. Program backed by peer-reviewed CSIRO research, including a 5-year community cohort evaluation."
- Mayo Clinic Diet: "Built on 15 sustainable habits. Members lose 6–10 lbs in the first 2 weeks. Rated in 10 of 11 U.S. News Best Diets categories."

### Call to Action
- AU: Visit totalwellbeingdiet.com.au — free trial. "Try the program that 50,000 Australians trust."
- US: Visit diet.mayoclinic.org — "Start the Weight Loss Program the Mayo Clinic built."
- Both: "If this changes how you think about fasting, save it and share it with someone who needs to hear it."

### Why This Will Perform
The Cochrane Review is the highest-credibility scientific citation available in nutrition research. The IF reversal is a genuine news cycle (NPR, Fox News, Inc., ScienceDaily all covered it in February 2026), and the 60-day window before news fatigue sets in is closing. No competitor has responded to this finding with institutional authority. This is precisely the kind of credibility moment that the "trusted doctor who is also a good friend" brand voice was built for.

### Production Notes
- **TIMING IS CRITICAL.** This brief should be prioritised for production this week. The 60-day news hook window is active but not indefinite.
- The dietitian's tone on the validation moment is crucial: warm, not triumphant. "The science has spoken and we've always been on the right side of it — and that's good news for you."
- On-screen text: display "22 studies" and "~2,000 participants" as the dietitian speaks — specific numbers anchor credibility and drive saves.
- No need for complex B-roll. Dietitian to camera is the right format.
- This concept pairs naturally with a follow-up piece: "So what does the evidence say you SHOULD do instead?" — plan both pieces in the same shoot.
- For Facebook, add a closing line: "What diet approach have you tried? Tell us in the comments." Comment prompts substantially increase Facebook Reels reach.

## Brief 3: "Why Women Over 50 Lose Weight Differently — And What the Science Says to Do About It"

**Platform:** YouTube long-form (primary, 8–12 minutes); Facebook Reels (60-second cut-down); Instagram Reels (45-second cut-down); TikTok (45-second hook clip)
**Format:** Expert explainer — series anchor episode
**Target length:** 8–12 minutes (YouTube full version); 45–60 seconds (short-form cuts)
**Market:** Both — CSIRO TWD branding (AU), Mayo Clinic Diet branding (US)
**Audience:** Women aged 45–65 experiencing perimenopause or postmenopause weight changes. Have tried multiple diets without sustained success. Attribute failure to themselves rather than to biology. Motivated by health outcomes, not appearance.

### Hook

YOUTUBE OPENING:
"If you've been doing everything right — eating well, moving more — and still can't shift the weight after 50, this is not a willpower problem. Your hormones have changed the rules. And most diets were never designed for that."

SHORT-FORM HOOK:
ON SCREEN TEXT: "Why diets that worked at 35 stop working at 50"
SPOKEN: "It's not you. It's your biology — and a [CSIRO / Mayo Clinic] dietitian explains exactly what changed."

Alternative hooks to test:
1. "Postmenopausal women have up to 30% lower resting metabolic rate than premenopausal women of the same weight. That is not a motivation gap. That is a biology gap."
2. "If you're a woman over 50 and every diet has stopped working — this video was made specifically for you, and it will change how you think about this."

### Video Structure (YouTube long-form, 8–12 minutes)

1. **Hook and credibility establishment (0–60 sec):** Dietitian to camera. State the problem plainly and warmly. Briefly establish credential.
2. **The biology of perimenopause and weight (60 sec – 3:30 min):** Plain-language explanation: declining oestrogen and fat distribution; muscle mass loss from the 40s (sarcopenia); metabolic rate changes; insulin sensitivity shifts; disrupted sleep and its effect on hunger hormones (leptin, ghrelin). Keep it grounded: "Here's what this feels like in your body, and here's the science behind why."
3. **Why standard diets fail for this group (3:30 – 5:30 min):** Most programs were designed on research conducted primarily in younger populations. Low-calorie diets without adequate protein accelerate muscle loss. Fasting approaches can worsen cortisol response in perimenopausal women. Points-based systems don't account for macronutrient quality. Name these failure modes without naming specific competitors.
4. **What the science says actually works (5:30 – 8:30 min):** Three pillars: (a) Higher protein intake — protects muscle mass, reduces hunger, supports metabolism. Give a practical number. (b) Low-GI eating — manages insulin sensitivity. Practical examples. (c) Structured support — accountability tools (AI coach, dietitian access, community) produce better outcomes than willpower alone. Reference program-specific tools (Hope AI coach for AU; Habit Optimizer for US).
5. **Real results from real members (8:30 – 10:00 min):** 1–2 member quotes from women in the 50+ cohort. Specific numbers — weight lost, health markers, energy. Avoid before/after physical framing. Focus on how they feel.
6. **The new research on menopause and weight loss medications (10:00 – 11:00 min):** Reference the 2026 study on tirzepatide + HRT outcomes for postmenopausal women. Not a medication pitch — context demonstrating research attention on this demographic. [US only: mention Weight-Loss Medications Program.] [AU: mention CSIRO TWD complements any medical approach your doctor recommends.]
7. **CTA and close (11:00 – 12:00 min):** Warm, direct. Program free trial link. "I'd also love to know in the comments — what's been the hardest part of managing your weight after 50? I read every comment."

### Key Messages
- Perimenopausal and postmenopausal weight changes are biological, not behavioural. Not a willpower failure.
- Standard diets (low-calorie, fasting-focused, points-based) were not designed for the hormonal and metabolic changes of midlife.
- Higher protein intake, low-GI eating, and structured behavioural support are the three evidence-backed levers that work for this group.
- CSIRO TWD and Mayo Clinic Diet are built on exactly this science.
- This audience is not niche — they are the largest, most motivated, most underserved group in the weight loss category.

### Science / Credibility Anchors
- 2026 study: postmenopausal women combining tirzepatide + HRT lost significantly more weight — evidence of growing research attention on this demographic
- Joint advisory (ACLM, ASN, Obesity Medicine Assoc, Obesity Society, 2026): 1.2–1.6g protein/kg/day — applicable to muscle preservation in midlife
- CSIRO TWD: "Higher-protein, low-GI framework backed by peer-reviewed CSIRO research. Average 7.2% body weight loss in 12 weeks. 5-year community cohort evaluation."
- Mayo Clinic Diet: "Built on 15 sustainable habits across 5 medically backed pillars. Rated in 10 of 11 U.S. News Best Diets categories."
- Note: all claims about menopause physiology should be reviewed by a clinical advisor before publication.

### Call to Action
- YouTube: "Start a free trial of the [CSIRO Total Wellbeing Diet / Mayo Clinic Diet] — link in the description. Subscribe for more content like this."
- Short-form: "Link in bio — free trial." Plus: "Save this to share with someone who needs to hear it."
- Facebook: "What's been your experience with weight loss after menopause? We read every comment."

### Why This Will Perform
This concept exploits the single clearest competitive gap in the research: no competitor is serving perimenopausal and postmenopausal women with credentialed science-backed content. Kic avoids weight framing. Juniper is TGA-restricted. WW has no science voice. Noom has no AU presence and no 40–55 content. Reverse Health is pursuing this demographic but without the institutional credibility anchor of CSIRO or Mayo Clinic. The keyword data shows "perimenopause belly fat" and "menopause belly fat" are consistently high-volume and rising. The 1-billion-views scale of #menopause content on TikTok confirms the demand.

### Production Notes
- **Series anchor.** Plan at minimum 4 follow-up episodes: Episode 2 — "The protein number women over 50 actually need"; Episode 3 — "Why sleep is the missing piece of midlife weight loss"; Episode 4 — "Exercise after menopause — what the research says (it's not what you think)."
- The dietitian presenter should ideally be a woman in the 40–55 age range for maximum audience identification.
- Background: warm kitchen or home-office setting — not a clinic. "Trusted friend who happens to have a science degree," not "medical appointment."
- On-screen text: use generously for statistics and the three evidence-backed pillars.
- B-roll ideas: hands preparing a high-protein meal; a woman walking outdoors; simple graphics showing hormonal changes; member community screenshots (with permission).
- The comment question at the end is not optional — it is a deliberate engagement mechanism. This audience will respond with genuine, detailed answers that also function as social proof and future content research.
- For the Facebook version, open with the validation line immediately and cut the biology explanation to two sentences — Facebook Reels requires the emotional hook to land within 3 seconds for the 50+ audience.
