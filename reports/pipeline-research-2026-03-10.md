**Topic 1: GLP-1 Medications — Expanding Universe, Expanding Anxiety**

Why it's trending: This is the dominant health story of early 2026 across both markets. The Wegovy pill received FDA approval in December 2025 and is now widely available in the US. Eli Lilly has secured Most-Favored-Nation pricing agreements with the Trump administration, bringing injectable costs to $245/month for Medicare. A landmark study from late February 2026 — the largest of its kind — found that GLP-1 drugs also protect against addiction across all major substances, generating enormous media coverage. Approximately 1 in 5 US adults have now used a GLP-1 drug, and 3 million Canadians are currently on them. Reddit communities are flooded with questions about side effects, what to eat, and how to stop losing muscle.

Relevance to Digital Wellness: This is our highest-priority strategic topic. The Mayo Clinic Diet already has a dedicated Weight-Loss Medications Program. The CSIRO TWD Eli Lilly PSP pilot puts AU squarely in this space. No competitor can match our clinical authority here.

Suggested content hook: "You've started a GLP-1. Now what? The science-backed eating plan that makes medication work harder" — anchored to CSIRO or Mayo Clinic research, positioning our programs as the essential structure layer alongside medication.

**Topic 2: Intermittent Fasting — The Science Has Spoken (and It's Not Flattering)**

Why it's trending: A landmark Cochrane review published February 2026 — 22 randomised clinical trials, nearly 2,000 participants — found that intermittent fasting produces "little to no difference" in weight loss compared to standard dietary advice. Coverage ran across NPR, Fox News, Inc., and ScienceDaily. A separate German study confirmed no metabolic benefit from time-restricted eating when calories remain equal. This is generating strong engagement in health communities as people who tried IF feel validated, and IF advocates push back.

Relevance to Digital Wellness: This is a credibility moment. CSIRO and Mayo Clinic both rely on energy balance and food quality — not timing gimmicks. We can own the "what the peer-reviewed evidence actually shows" angle in a way no influencer or competitor can credibly claim.

Suggested content hook: "The biggest study on intermittent fasting just dropped. Here's what a CSIRO/Mayo Clinic dietitian actually thinks it means for your weight loss" — reactive, timely, authoritative, and shareable.

**Topic 3: Muscle Loss on GLP-1s — The Conversation Mainstream Media Is Just Catching Up On**

Why it's trending: Emerging clinical data in early 2026 confirms that up to 40% of weight lost on high-efficacy GLP-1 medications may be lean muscle mass. A joint advisory from the American College of Lifestyle Medicine, American Society for Nutrition, the Obesity Medicine Association, and the Obesity Society (published in early 2026) now recommends 1.2–1.6g of protein per kg of body weight for GLP-1 users — yet research shows only 43% of GLP-1 users are meeting the minimum threshold. Harvard Science Review ran a feature in February 2026. This topic is surging on Reddit (r/Ozempic, r/WeightLoss), with users sharing concerns about looking "smaller but not healthier" and asking how to protect muscle.

Relevance to Digital Wellness: This is a content gap with our name on it. The CSIRO TWD is already a high-protein, structured program. The Mayo Clinic Diet's Weight-Loss Medications Program explicitly includes higher-protein meal plans and a resistance exercise plan designed for GLP-1 users. We have the research credibility and the product to address this directly.

Suggested content hook: "Up to 40% of what you lose on Ozempic might be muscle. Here's the protein and exercise protocol that changes that" — backed by Mayo Clinic or CSIRO dietitian expertise.

**Topic 4: Perimenopause and Menopause Weight Loss — A Rapidly Growing Audience Signal**

Why it's trending: 2026 is being described in women's health media as a "threshold year" — a shift from managing menopause symptoms to proactive, life-stage-specific care. A study published early 2026 found that postmenopausal women combining tirzepatide with hormone replacement therapy lost significantly more weight than those on medication alone. Searches for "weight loss after menopause," "perimenopause belly fat," and "why am I gaining weight in my 40s" are consistently high-volume and rising. Platforms like Reverse Health are explicitly targeting the 40+ female demographic and growing fast.

Relevance to Digital Wellness: Our existing 50+ female audience is the core menopause demographic. The 35–50 expansion target maps directly onto the perimenopause demographic. CSIRO's high-protein, lower-GI model is scientifically well-suited to the hormonal and metabolic shifts of this life stage. This is an owned audience we are not fully speaking to with life-stage-specific language.

Suggested content hook: "Why the diet that worked in your 30s isn't working anymore — and what CSIRO research shows actually does" — targeted at women 40–55.

**Topic 5: Gut Health and "Fibermaxxing" — Moving From Trend to Mainstream**

Why it's trending: "Fibermaxxing" — the practice of systematically increasing dietary fibre intake and variety, including the "30 plants a week" principle — is the breakout nutrition trend of early 2026. Coverage is extensive in both AU and US health media. The global digestive health products market is now worth over $51 billion. Australian searches for high-fibre foods, prebiotic products, and gut-health snacks are tracking upward. The link between gut health, metabolic health, blood sugar regulation, and weight loss is being surfaced in mainstream content, not just specialist publications.

Relevance to Digital Wellness: The CSIRO TWD's high-fibre, low-GI model is directly aligned with the science behind gut health and metabolic eating. This is an opportunity to re-present an existing program feature through a modern, trending lens — showing that what we've been doing for 20 years is what the science now confirms.

Suggested content hook: "Why the CSIRO TWD was already a gut-health diet before gut health was trending" — an evergreen reframe that shows scientific prescience and builds credibility with a younger, health-literate audience.

## 3. Platform Trends

### TikTok / Instagram Reels

**Winning format:** Expert-led myth-busting, delivered in 30–60 seconds by a credentialed professional (registered dietitian, clinician, or scientist). The format follows a simple structure: make a provocative or counterintuitive claim in the first two seconds, deliver the evidence-backed explanation, and close with a practical takeaway. Research published in 2026 confirms that dietitian-created content is the most accurate and highest quality on the platform, yet accounts for only 9% of total nutrition posts. This is the gap Digital Wellness should step into.

**Trending hooks that are performing:**
- "I'm a [dietitian / Mayo Clinic / CSIRO] and here's what the study actually found..."
- "The most common question I get from people on Ozempic is..."
- "This is what happens to your muscle when you stop eating enough protein on weight loss medication"
- "3 things nobody tells you about losing weight after 40"
- "The diet I've been recommending for 20 years is now the gut health diet"

**Content styles working now:**
- Science myth-busting (e.g., the IF backlash is live content gold right now)
- "Day in my diet" / "What I eat in a day" — particularly for GLP-1 users, high protein formats
- Before/after storytelling anchored to behaviour change, not aesthetics
- Accountability and daily check-in formats (drives comments, return views)
- POV videos ("POV: You're a dietitian who actually looked at the Ozempic research")
- Exercise snack content — short resistance training demonstrations positioned as muscle preservation for GLP-1 users

**Important platform consideration:** Facebook and Instagram have policies restricting weight loss promotion content. Organic content must avoid implying negative self-perception. All content should be framed around health outcomes and science — which aligns perfectly with our brand voice and provides a structural advantage over competitors who rely on before/after transformation content.

### YouTube

**Winning format:** (1) Long-form expert explainers anchored to news cycles or scientific findings (8–18 minutes) and (2) YouTube Shorts (under 60 seconds) functioning as awareness/discovery content that feeds subscribers to longer videos.

**Content length sweet spot:** 8–15 minutes for the core authority content format. For GLP-1 and menopause topics, 15–20 minute deep-dives are performing well, as these audiences have high purchase intent and will watch longer for trustworthy guidance.

**Growing content types:**

## 4. GLP-1 Landscape

### Current Sentiment & News

The first quarter of 2026 marks a decisive phase shift in the GLP-1 story — from "emerging trend" to structural market reality. The FDA approved the first oral GLP-1 (semaglutide pill, under the Wegovy brand) on December 22, 2025, and it became widely commercially available in the United States in March 2026. Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly have both secured Most-Favored-Nation pricing deals with the Trump administration, reducing injectable costs to $245/month for Medicare patients and setting a $150/month benchmark for out-of-pocket pill pricing. These pricing shifts are expected to meaningfully expand the addressable user base in the US throughout 2026.

In Australia, GLP-1 drugs remain unapproved by the PBS for weight loss (they are PBS-listed only for type 2 diabetes). The private cost of Mounjaro in Australia starts at $395/month, and Wegovy at $460/dose — making them accessible only to higher-income individuals. The TGA's October 2024 crackdown on compounded GLP-1 products eliminated access for many who had been using cheaper pharmacy-compounded "replicas," generating resentment and frustration in online communities. The net result in Australia is a large population who are interested in, aware of, or adjacent to GLP-1 medications, but who cannot access or afford them — creating a direct and tangible audience for structured diet programs that deliver real results without medication.

Eli Lilly's next-generation retatrutide (a triple hormone agonist targeting GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon) reported Phase 3 results showing 29% body weight loss — more than any currently marketed drug — and is expected to seek FDA approval by year's end.

### Top Questions People Are Asking About GLP-1

1. "What should I eat on Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro?" — The single highest-intent GLP-1 content question online. The Mayo Clinic Diet and CSIRO TWD have a direct, ownable, science-backed answer that no competitor can replicate.
2. "Will I lose muscle on Ozempic?" — Rapidly growing query. A Harvard Science Review piece in February 2026 and a joint advisory from four major US clinical societies brought this to public attention.
3. "How much protein do I need on weight loss medication?" — 43% of GLP-1 users are not meeting minimum protein recommendations. A dietitian-backed, specific, actionable answer is a category-winning content opportunity.
4. "What happens when you stop taking Ozempic?" — One of the most-searched GLP-1 questions. Directly relevant to the "program as sustainability layer" angle.
5. "Is there a diet plan for GLP-1 users?" — Commercial intent, directly answered by the Mayo Clinic Diet Weight-Loss Medications Program.
6. "What are the side effects of Ozempic and how do I manage them?" — Diet and eating patterns are directly relevant to managing nausea, GI issues, and fatigue on GLP-1s.
7. "Are GLP-1 drugs available in Australia?" / "How much does Ozempic cost in Australia?" — High-frustration queries in the AU market. Content that contextualises availability, cost, and alternatives captures a motivated but medication-inaccessible audience.
8. "Can I use diet alone instead of Ozempic?" — Strong CSIRO TWD acquisition content opportunity.

### Content Gap: GLP-1 Companion Programs

The most significant gap in the current GLP-1 content market is authoritative, structured, science-backed guidance on what to actually do while taking these medications — specifically: how to eat to preserve muscle mass, how to meet protein targets when appetite is suppressed, how to build sustainable habits so that weight is not regained when the medication is stopped or reduced, and how to incorporate resistance exercise alongside medication-driven weight loss.

The market is currently served by generic content (meal planning blogs, pharmaceutical company FAQs, telehealth startup guides), none of which carry clinical authority. Optavia has launched a "GLP-1 Lifestyle Program" in partnership with LifeMD, signalling that the commercial category is being formed now. WW has been pivoting toward GLP-1 users for approximately 18 months.

The gap that Digital Wellness can own — and that no competitor can credibly claim — is the "clinically authoritative structured companion program" position. The Mayo Clinic Diet already has this product: the Weight-Loss Medications Program explicitly includes a higher-protein meal plan, a resistance exercise plan, and a year-long structure for developing sustainable habits. In Australia, the CSIRO TWD + Eli Lilly PSP pilot is building toward exactly this.

The specific content products that are missing and that Digital Wellness could create: a "GLP-1 user's guide to protein" (Mayo Clinic and CSIRO dietitian-authored), a "12-week muscle preservation plan for weight loss medication users" (resistance exercise + high-protein meal plan, downloadable), a "what to eat in your first week on Ozempic" video series, and a "when you stop the medication, here's what keeps the weight off" long-form content series aimed at the post-medication retention audience.

## 5. Strategic Summary

### Top 3 Immediate Content Opportunities

**1. Own the "GLP-1 Companion" Content Category — Now, Before Competitors Consolidate**

The window to establish category leadership in science-backed GLP-1 companion content is open right now and will not remain open for long. WW and Optavia are already moving. The Mayo Clinic Diet has the product; CSIRO TWD has the protein-forward program and the Eli Lilly pilot in motion. The content strategy should immediately produce a dedicated hub of GLP-1-specific content — covering protein targets, muscle preservation, side effect management, and sustainability after medication — attributed to Mayo Clinic and CSIRO dietitians, distributed across YouTube (long-form), Reels and TikTok (short-form myth-busting and how-to), and anchored by SEO-optimised articles targeting the highest-intent GLP-1 queries.

**2. Capitalise on the Intermittent Fasting Reversal as a Credibility Moment**

The February 2026 Cochrane review is a live news cycle that will remain relevant in health conversations for the next 60–90 days. This is a rare circumstance where the science directly validates what Digital Wellness has always stood for — a structured, food-quality-first, evidence-based approach to sustainable weight loss. An immediate content response — a Mayo Clinic or CSIRO dietitian on camera responding to the study, a short explainer article, a "what the science actually says" Reel — will generate earned reach, social sharing, and SEO traffic at a moment when the topic is highly searched. The shelf life of the news hook is short (60 days). The evergreen article version of this content has indefinite value.

**3. Build a Dedicated Perimenopause and Menopause Weight Loss Content Pillar**

The 35–50 audience expansion goal and the existing 50+ core audience converge directly on the perimenopause/menopause weight loss topic. This is a high-volume, high-frustration, underserved content area where CSIRO and Mayo Clinic's scientific authority is exactly what searchers are looking for. A structured content pillar — covering hormonal drivers of weight gain, protein and macronutrient needs at this life stage, the role of resistance training, and the evidence behind specific dietary approaches — would serve both audience segments simultaneously. It is also an area where the competitors most threatening to Digital Wellness's 35–50 expansion (Reverse Health, Juniper, Kic) are producing content that is heavy on personal story and light on clinical evidence.

### Audience Signals

**35–50 audience:** This cohort is actively seeking weight loss information that acknowledges the specific challenges of their life stage — hormonal shifts, muscle loss, metabolic slowdown — and delivers science-backed answers rather than generic calorie advice. They are searching for perimenopause and menopause content at increasing rates, are the primary GLP-1 user demographic, and show a strong preference for credentialed expertise over influencer recommendation. They use TikTok and Instagram for discovery but convert via longer-form content and program trust. The CSIRO and Mayo Clinic brand names have strong recognition in this cohort — particularly the Mayo Clinic in the US — but our content presence in their language (hormonal, metabolic, life-stage-specific) is currently limited.

**50+ audience:** Continues to be motivated primarily by health outcomes (managing chronic conditions, reducing medication dependence, improving energy and mobility) rather than aesthetic goals. They are more likely to be on or adjacent to GLP-1 medications for diabetes or cardiovascular risk, and they are the demographic most at risk of the muscle loss problem associated with rapid weight loss. They respond strongly to member stories that reflect their own demographic, to scientific authority framing, and to practical, structured guidance. Facebook remains the primary organic channel for this group. The 50,000-member CSIRO TWD community is an underleveraged asset for this purpose.
